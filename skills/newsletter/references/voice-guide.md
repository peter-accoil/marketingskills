# Newsletter Voice Guide

This guide captures Peter's newsletter voice and style based on actual examples and the product marketing context.

## Core Voice Characteristics

**Casual and friendly**
- Opens with "Howdy y'all 👋"
- Uses conversational language like "nearly a wrap", "rock'n'roll"
- Writes in first person as Peter
- Feels like a personal email, not a corporate announcement

**Clear and helpful**
- Explains what features are in plain language
- Breaks down who each feature is for
- Provides concrete use cases and examples
- Avoids jargon and buzzwords

**Organized and scannable**
- Clear section headings for each major feature
- "Who's this for?" subheadings
- Bulleted lists for minor updates
- Logical flow from big announcements to smaller items to what's next

**Action-oriented**
- Includes calls to action where relevant
- Links to relevant pages or resources
- Invites feedback and questions
- Encourages readers to try features or book demos

## Voice Patterns

### Opening
```
Howdy y'all 👋

[Month YYYY] is [nearly a wrap / here / wrapping up / etc]. Here's what landed in [Month], including [exciting teaser].
```

**Examples:**
- "February is nearly a wrap."
- "March is here, and we shipped some big updates."
- "January flew by — here's what we built."

### Feature Introductions
Start with clear, benefit-focused headlines, then explain what it is:

```
[Feature Name or Benefit]
[What it is and what it does, written conversationally]
```

**Examples:**
- "AI Account Summaries" (feature name)
- "See what changed in any account — instantly" (benefit)

### "Who's this for?" Sections
Be specific about the audience and use case:

```
Who's this for?
[Specific personas, use cases, and how they'd use it]
```

**Examples:**
- "Who's this for? Customer Success teams who need to quickly understand account health before renewal conversations."
- "Who's this for? Product teams tracking activation progress for trial users."

### Minor Updates Section
```
A few smaller things
- [Update 1 - one line]
- [Update 2 - one line]
- [Update 3 - one line]
```

Keep these concise — one line per item. No need for full explanations.

### "What's Next" Section
```
What's next
[1-2 paragraphs about upcoming focus areas, features in pipeline, areas of investment]
```

This section is optional. Only include if there's something meaningful to share.

### Closing
```
On behalf of the whole Accoil team: [warm closing message]

Peter
```

**Examples:**
- "On behalf of the whole Accoil team: thanks for being part of this journey. We're building Accoil for you, so if you have feedback or ideas, we'd love to hear from you."
- "On behalf of the whole Accoil team: we appreciate your trust and feedback as we keep building. Rock'n'roll."

## What Peter's Voice Sounds Like

✅ **Do this:**
- "We shipped a big one this month"
- "This one's for teams who..."
- "You can now..."
- "Here's how it works"
- "We're excited about this because..."
- "Thanks for being part of this journey"

❌ **Not this:**
- "We are pleased to announce..."
- "This innovative solution delivers..."
- "Leveraging advanced algorithms..."
- "Best-in-class functionality..."
- "Stakeholders will benefit from..."
- "Revolutionary new platform capabilities..."

## Tone Calibration

**How casual is too casual?**
- ✅ "Howdy y'all" - Yes, this is Peter's style
- ✅ "rock'n'roll" - Yes, as a sign-off
- ✅ Emoji in greeting (👋) - Yes, sparingly
- ❌ Excessive emojis throughout - No
- ❌ Slang or internet-speak - No
- ❌ Memes or GIFs - No

**How technical should it be?**
- Explain features in plain language
- Avoid acronyms unless they're industry-standard (CRM, API)
- If you use a technical term, explain what it means in practice
- Focus on what the feature does, not how it's built

**How sales-y should it be?**
- Include CTAs where they make sense (try feature, book demo, give feedback)
- Don't oversell or use hype language
- Be honest about what features do and who they're for
- Trust readers to recognize value without pressure

- "A quick look at what's new"

**Introducing features:**

## Before/After Examples

### Example 1: Feature Announcement

❌ **Before (too formal):**
```
We are pleased to announce the release of our new AI-powered account summary functionality. This innovative feature leverages advanced natural language processing to deliver comprehensive insights into account health and engagement patterns.
```

✅ **After (Peter's voice):**
```
AI Account Summaries

We built AI summaries for every account. Instead of digging through dashboards, you get a plain-language summary of what changed, why it matters, and what to do about it.

Who's this for?
Customer Success teams who need to quickly understand account health before calls or check-ins. Instead of spending 10 minutes reviewing dashboards, you get the context in 30 seconds.
```

### Example 2: Minor Update

❌ **Before (too wordy):**
```
- Enhanced our platform's notification system with improved filtering capabilities that enable users to more effectively manage and customize their alert preferences based on specific criteria and conditions.
```

✅ **After (concise):**
```
- Better notification filters — control exactly which alerts you see
```

### Example 3: Closing

❌ **Before (corporate):**
```
We remain committed to delivering best-in-class solutions that drive measurable business outcomes for our valued customers. Please don't hesitate to reach out with any questions or feedback.

Sincerely,
The Accoil Team
```

✅ **After (Peter's voice):**
```
On behalf of the whole Accoil team: thanks for being part of this journey. We're building Accoil for you, so if you have feedback or ideas, we'd love to hear from you.

Peter
```

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing the newsletter, check:

- [ ] Opens with "Howdy y'all 👋"
- [ ] Written in first person from Peter
- [ ] Each major feature has "Who's this for?" section
- [ ] Language is conversational and clear (no jargon)
- [ ] Minor updates are concise (one line each)
- [ ] Includes relevant links and CTAs
- [ ] Ends with warm, personal closing
- [ ] Signs as "Peter"
- [ ] Overall tone feels helpful, not sales-y
- [ ] Sounds like Peter talking to customers, not a press release
